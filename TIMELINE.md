# Timeline

This document preserves major milestones, realizations, and directional shifts that influenced the repositories, research efforts, and investigations associated with Steven Kyle Hensley.

Dates are approximate where exact records are unavailable.

---

## Beginning

### Curiosity Before Repositories

Before repositories, frameworks, architecture discussions, or formal research efforts, there was curiosity.

I spent time reading public discussions, technical posts, research threads, and debates across online communities, particularly LinkedIn.

At the time I had little formal knowledge of many of the subjects being discussed.

I was not a trained architect.

I was not a security professional.

I was not a computer scientist.

I was simply trying to understand what people were talking about.

Instead of remaining a spectator, I started asking questions.

Some questions exposed gaps in my understanding.

Others challenged assumptions I did not yet realize were widely accepted.

The goal was never to become an expert.

The goal was to understand.

---

## Discovering GitHub

Shortly after becoming active in public discussions, I began learning GitHub.

Initially GitHub was simply a place to explore projects, discussions, repositories, and ideas.

Over time it became something more.

A place to document learning.

A place to preserve ideas.

A place to test assumptions.

A place to record investigations.

Many early repositories were learning exercises rather than finished systems.

Looking back, they document the path as much as the destination.

---

## Learning in Public

As participation increased, I found myself engaging with people whose backgrounds far exceeded my own.

Researchers.

Architects.

Engineers.

The experience was often uncomfortable.

There was always more to learn.

There was always another subject I did not understand.

## DTPE Era

Research increasingly focused on questions surrounding:

- Decision accountability
- Policy evidence
- Verification
- Trust systems
- Explainability
- Recoverability

This period contributed to the development of:

**DTPE**
(Deterministic Trust & Policy Evidence)

A recurring question emerged:

> Can we prove a decision followed policy?

The focus shifted away from what systems claim and toward what systems can prove.

---

## Continuity Realization

Repeated participation in discussions, projects, AI conversations, documentation efforts, and repository development revealed a recurring problem.

Information drift.

Observed forms included:

- Context drift
- Documentation drift
- Memory drift
- Direction drift
- Project drift
- Assumption drift

Knowledge was constantly mutating as it moved between people, conversations, summaries, repositories, and implementations.

This led to a foundational realization:

> The repository itself must become the source of truth.

Not only for code.

For assumptions.

For intent.

For decisions.

For continuity.

For evidence.

For understanding.

---

## Emergence of BBIS

As continuity questions matured, a deeper question appeared:

> Can a governing invariant survive every mutation-capable boundary until the irreversible mutation authority?

The question was simple.

The implications were not.

This eventually became known as:

**Boundary-to-Boundary Invariant Survival (BBIS)**

The investigation expanded beyond trust and accountability into survivability itself.

# Principles

This document records the recurring principles that emerged through investigation, experimentation, failure, revision, and public discussion.

These principles are not presented as universal truths.

They are observations that repeatedly survived testing.

---

## Principle 1: Claims Are Not Evidence

A claim may be true.

A claim may be false.

The claim itself proves neither.

Evidence exists independently of belief.

---

## Principle 2: Evidence Is Not Verification

Possessing evidence does not automatically establish correctness.

Evidence must be examined, challenged, interpreted, and validated.

Verification requires process.

---

## Principle 3: Verification Is Not Truth

Verification increases confidence.

It does not guarantee truth.

All verification operates within boundaries, assumptions, and available information.

---

## Principle 4: Truth Survives Execution Failure

Systems fail.

Implementations fail.

Organizations fail.

People fail.

Truth remains true regardless of failure.

The objective is not merely execution.

The objective is preserving recoverable truth.

---

## Principle 5: Continuity Is A System Property

Memory is unreliable.

Documentation becomes outdated.

Conversations drift.

Understanding changes.

If continuity matters, continuity must be intentionally designed into the system.

---

## Principle 6: Repositories Are More Than Code

A repository can preserve:

- Intent
- Assumptions
- Decisions
- Direction
- Context
- Evidence
- Understanding

Code alone rarely explains itself.

---

## Principle 7: Questions Are Valuable

Many important discoveries begin as questions.

The absence of certainty is not a weakness.

Investigation begins where certainty ends.

---

## Principle 8: Understanding Before Authority

Authority can influence acceptance.

It cannot replace understanding.

The goal is not to win arguments.

The goal is to understand what is true.

---

## Principle 9: Preserve Lineage

Ideas do not emerge fully formed.

They evolve.

Understanding where an idea came from is often as important as understanding its final form.

Lineage preserves context.

---

## Principle 10: Survivability Matters

A system should not be judged solely by how it behaves under ideal conditions.

The more important question is:

What survives when conditions are no longer ideal?

---

## Principle 11: The Boundary Matters

Every transfer introduces risk.

Every boundary introduces transformation.

Every transformation creates the possibility of loss.

Understanding a system requires understanding its boundaries.

---

## Principle 12: Investigation Is Ongoing

Every conclusion is a snapshot.

Every repository is a snapshot.

Every model is a snapshot.

Learning does not end because documentation exists.

The investigation continues.

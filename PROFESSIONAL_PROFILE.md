# Steven Kyle Hensley

## Security Architecture Researcher

I investigate whether security and governance claims survive
distributed execution.

My work focuses on:

- Adversarial architecture analysis
- Distributed authorization
- Governance invariants
- Evidence and provenance
- Agentic execution
- Mutation-capable boundaries
- Security claim falsification
- Reproducible security experiments

## Research Method

I use a repeatable adversarial workflow:

    CLAIM
      ↓
    RECONSTRUCT
      ↓
    FORMALIZE
      ↓
    ATTACK
      ↓
    COUNTEREXAMPLE
      ↓
    DESIGN CONTROL
      ↓
    BUILD TEST
      ↓
    MEASURE
      ↓
    REVISE

I am particularly interested in security properties that appear
correct at individual components but fail when those components
are composed.

## Current Research

### Boundary-to-Boundary Invariant Survival (BBIS)

Investigates whether a governing invariant can survive every
mutation-capable boundary until the effect-relative irreversible
mutation authority.

### Active Invariant Cloning (AIC)

Investigates whether a governing invariant can be actively
instantiated across multiple enforcement boundaries while
preserving mutation identity, authorization lineage, and
refusal capability.

### Deterministic Trust & Policy Evidence (DTPE)

Investigates whether distributed decisions can retain
deterministic, verifiable evidence of the policy and authority
state under which they were executed.

## Working Philosophy

I do not begin by assuming a security claim is true.

I try to break it.

If the claim survives the attack, I try to break the
remaining assumptions.

If it fails, I document the failure and determine the smallest
architectural condition required to prevent it.

Evidence comes before advocacy.

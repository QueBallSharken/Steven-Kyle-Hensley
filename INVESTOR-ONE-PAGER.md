# THE OPPORTUNITY

AI systems are increasingly capable of taking actions across multiple
agents, tools, services, APIs, hosts, and physical interfaces.

The security problem is no longer simply:

"Was this action authorized?"

A harder question is:

"Did the condition that made the action legitimate remain enforceable
as the action crossed every boundary leading to execution?"

This research investigates that problem.

Central research question:

Can a governing invariant survive every mutation-capable boundary until
the irreversible mutation authority?

# BBIS

## Boundary-to-Boundary Invariant Survival

BBIS examines whether a governing condition remains capable of refusing
execution across every mutation-capable boundary.

Core distinction:

    Historical approval != live governance

An authorization record can prove that something was approved.

It does not necessarily prove that the approval remains enforceable
at the point where an irreversible action occurs.

BBIS turns this question into a testable architectural property.

---

# AIC

## Active Invariant Cloning

AIC investigates whether governing invariants can be actively reproduced
at execution boundaries rather than merely inherited from upstream
components.

Core principle:

    No inherited authority.
    Recompute authority at the boundary.

The objective is to prevent downstream execution from treating an
upstream assertion as automatically authoritative.

---

# DTPE

## Deterministic Trust & Policy Evidence

DTPE investigates deterministic evidence for answering:

    Did the decision actually follow the applicable policy?

The architecture focuses on preserving relevant:

- policy state
- authority state
- decision inputs
- execution context
- canonical snapshots
- receipts

The goal is reproducible evidence rather than retrospective interpretation.

---

# IAL

## Intent & Accountability Layer

IAL investigates binding declared intent and accountability information
to governed execution.

The objective is to reduce ambiguity around:

- who requested an action
- what was intended
- what authority was claimed
- what policy applied
- what decision was actually executed

---

# IRONCLAD

## IRONCLAD Echo System

IRONCLAD is a defensive governance reference implementation combining
multiple elements of the research.

Current implementation includes:

- BBIS-oriented boundary verification
- invariant recomputation
- intent requirements
- quorum-controlled privileged approval
- deterministic decision receipts
- schema validation
- canonical snapshots
- snapshot-boundary checks
- SafeBox decoy containment
- nearby/RF ingress event handling
- environmental interference decisions
- compromised-host fail-safe logic
- non-destructive host observation
- receipt export

IRONCLAD is currently a local research verifier and test harness.

It is NOT being represented as production-grade enforcement.

# Glossary

This document records recurring terminology used throughout repositories, discussions, research efforts, and continuity artifacts.

Definitions may evolve over time.

Where ambiguity exists, historical usage should be preserved.

---

## BBIS

Boundary-to-Boundary Invariant Survival.

The investigation into whether a governing invariant can survive every mutation-capable boundary until the irreversible mutation authority.

---

## Invariant

A property, requirement, rule, constraint, or governing condition intended to remain unchanged despite system evolution, transformation, translation, or execution.

---

## Boundary

Any location where information, authority, interpretation, execution, control, or representation may change.

Examples include:

- Systems
- Organizations
- Teams
- Repositories
- APIs
- Processes
- Humans
- AI models

---

## Mutation

Any alteration occurring as information passes through a boundary.

Mutation may be:

- Intentional
- Accidental
- Beneficial
- Harmful
- Observable
- Hidden

---

## Mutation Authority

The first boundary capable of irreversibly altering an invariant.

Identification of mutation authority is a recurring area of investigation.

---

## Ghost

Informal term.

Represents the dependency one layer beyond current visibility.

The dependency behind the dependency.

The authority behind the authority.

The assumption behind the assumption.

Originally described as:

> "I want to put the invariant in my pocket and slap the ghost when he shows up."

---

## Continuity

The ability for understanding, intent, evidence, governance, identity, or truth to survive across time and transformation.

---

## Continuity Artifact

Any preserved object intended to maintain continuity.

Examples:

- Repositories
- Documentation
- Receipts
- Handoff files
- Specifications
- Evidence records

---

## Source of Truth

The authoritative location from which understanding should be reconstructed.

A recurring realization throughout multiple investigations was:

> Repositories must become the source of truth.

---

## DTPE

Deterministic Trust & Policy Evidence.

An investigation into proving whether decisions followed governing policy.

Associated question:

> Can we prove a decision followed policy?

---

## Recoverability

The ability to reconstruct truth after failure, loss, mutation, compromise, or disagreement.

---

## Governance Survivability

The ability of governance mechanisms to remain effective despite system evolution, personnel changes, implementation changes, or environmental disruption.

---

## LMA

Living Mycelium Architecture.

A continuity-first architectural model inspired by biological mycelium networks.

Associated principle:

> Truth survives even when execution does not.

---

## Investigation

The ongoing process.

Most repositories connected to this account are artifacts of investigations rather than final conclusions.

---

## Continuity Repository

A repository whose primary purpose is preserving understanding rather than delivering software.

---

## Repository Drift

The gradual divergence between repository contents and intended understanding.

Often caused by:

- Missing context
- Missing decisions
- Missing assumptions
- Missing continuity artifacts

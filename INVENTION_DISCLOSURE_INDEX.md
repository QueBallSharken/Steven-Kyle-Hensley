4. ID-001 — BOUNDARY-TO-BOUNDARY INVARIANT SURVIVAL
============================================================

INVENTION-ID:

ID-001

TITLE:

Boundary-to-Boundary Invariant Survival

SHORT NAME:

BBIS

PROBLEM:

Agentic and distributed execution systems may preserve authorization
records, policy references, delegation information, or approval history
while failing to preserve the actual governing condition that makes a
future mutation legitimate.

An upstream approval does not automatically establish that a downstream
mutation remains authorized.

A system may therefore appear governed while the final execution boundary
is no longer capable of refusing an invalid action.

CORE RESEARCH QUESTION:

Can a governing invariant survive every mutation-capable boundary until
the irreversible mutation authority?

TECHNICAL MECHANISM:

Candidate mechanisms include:

- identification of mutation-capable boundaries;
- canonical governing state;
- invariant propagation;
- invariant recomputation;
- local admissibility evaluation;
- refusal semantics;
- final-boundary enforcement;
- commit-point verification;
- alternate-path enforcement;
- retry-path enforcement;
- recovery-path enforcement;
- override-path enforcement;
- dumb-endpoint protection.

IMPLEMENTATION:

BBIS research and associated implementation work.

RELATED SYSTEMS:

- AIC
- DTPE
- IRONCLAD
- governance continuity research

DATE HISTORY:

Research developed through 2026 OWASP discussions and subsequent formal
research.

PUBLIC DISCLOSURE:

Relevant public discussion includes OWASP Issue #817 and associated
research documentation.

COLLABORATORS:

Potentially relevant collaborators must be evaluated according to their
actual contribution to any specific claimed invention.

Known research collaborators include:

- Bradley Bates
- Richard Porter

Their participation does not automatically establish inventorship for
BBIS or any later implementation.

CURRENT REPOSITORY:

Relevant research is distributed across the author's public repositories.

PRIMARY RESEARCH ANCHOR:

OWASP Issue #817.

TEST EVIDENCE:

BBIS stress-test concepts include:

- refusal at signal emission;
- independent commit-point checking;
- invariant tracing across delegation and translation;
- full mutation-path coverage;
- dumb-endpoint coverage.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS:

BBIS does not automatically establish:

- physical safety;
- instantaneous revocation;
- trustworthy hardware;
- uncompromised lower-layer infrastructure;
- correct physical-world interpretation;
- cryptographic security without implemented cryptography.

POTENTIAL CLAIM TERRITORY:

Requires professional patent analysis.

Potential technical focus:

A concrete mechanism for preserving and re-establishing an enforceable
governing condition across multiple mutation-capable execution boundaries
until an irreversible mutation point.

PRIOR-ART QUESTIONS:

- distributed authorization systems;
- policy enforcement points;
- zero-trust architectures;
- capability systems;
- transaction authorization;
- workflow approval systems;
- policy engines;
- reference monitors;
- distributed commit protocols;
- provenance systems.

PATENTABILITY REVIEW:

UNASSESSED

COMMERCIAL USE CASE:

POTENTIAL CUSTOMERS:

============================================================

INVENTION-ID:

ID-002

TITLE:

Active Invariant Cloning

SHORT NAME:

AIC

PROBLEM:

Copying an authorization field or historical approval artifact across
system boundaries does not necessarily preserve its ability to refuse
invalid execution.

A representation can survive while its governance semantics disappear.

TECHNICAL MECHANISM:

AIC investigates the reproduction of a governing invariant at downstream
execution boundaries while preserving:

- identity;
- authorization basis;
- admissibility;
- invalidation conditions;
- temporal validity;
- refusal semantics.

The central distinction is:

COPIED DATA

versus

ACTIVE GOVERNANCE.

IMPLEMENTATION:

AIC research repository and formal audit work.

DATE HISTORY:

PUBLIC DISCLOSURE:

6. ID-003 — DETERMINISTIC TRUST & POLICY EVIDENCE
============================================================

INVENTION-ID:

ID-003

TITLE:

Deterministic Trust & Policy Evidence

SHORT NAME:

DTPE

PROBLEM:

A system may claim that an action followed policy without preserving
enough information to independently reproduce or verify the decision.

Logs can show that a decision occurred.

They do not necessarily prove why the decision was permitted.

TECHNICAL MECHANISM:

DTPE investigates deterministic execution evidence based on:

- canonical policy state;
- authority state;
- policy snapshots;
- authority snapshots;
- deterministic evaluation;
- canonical decision representation;
- reproducible receipts;
- evidence binding.

CORE QUESTION:

Can we prove that a decision followed policy?

IMPLEMENTATION:

DTPE research and implementation work.

RELATED SYSTEMS:

- BBIS
- IRONCLAD
- governance evidence receipts

TEST EVIDENCE:

Deterministic receipt generation and canonicalization mechanisms are
implemented in related research artifacts.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS:

DTPE evidence does not automatically establish that the underlying
policy itself was correct.

It demonstrates decision consistency against declared inputs.

POTENTIAL CLAIM TERRITORY:

A concrete deterministic mechanism for binding a decision to a canonical
policy and authority state and producing reproducible evidence of that
evaluation.

PRIOR-ART QUESTIONS:

- audit logging;
- policy engines;
- signed attestations;
- transparency logs;
- provenance systems;
- authorization receipts;
- event sourcing.

PATENTABILITY REVIEW:

UNASSESSED

COMMERCIAL USE CASE:

Enterprise AI governance and compliance.

POTENTIAL CUSTOMERS:

============================================================

INVENTION-ID:

ID-010

TITLE:

Governance Continuity / Invariant Survival Property

PROBLEM:

In a non-atomic execution path, a safety predicate may be evaluated early
and then become irrelevant or unenforceable before the final mutation.

TECHNICAL CONCEPT:

intermediate mutation stages until the irreversible primitive.

without identifying a concrete technical implementation.

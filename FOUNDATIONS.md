# Foundations

This document records foundational observations that repeatedly influenced investigations, repositories, research efforts, and continuity systems.

These foundations are not conclusions.

They are recurring starting points.

---

## Foundation 1: Reality Exists Independently Of Description

Descriptions can be wrong.

Models can be wrong.

Documentation can be wrong.

Understanding can be wrong.

Reality remains unchanged.

The purpose of investigation is reducing the distance between description and reality.

---

## Foundation 2: Information Changes When It Moves

Information rarely survives transfer unchanged.

Movement introduces:

- Translation
- Interpretation
- Simplification
- Mutation
- Loss

Every boundary creates opportunity for change.

---

## Foundation 3: Every System Has Boundaries

Systems are often defined less by what they contain and more by where they end.

Boundaries determine:

- Authority
- Responsibility
- Mutation
- Trust
- Survivability

Understanding a system requires understanding its boundaries.

---

## Foundation 4: Survivability Reveals Structure

A thing's ability to survive often reveals more than its intended design.

Questions worth asking include:

- What survives?
- What changes?
- What disappears?
- What remains recoverable?

---

## Foundation 5: Trust Is Expensive

Trust requires assumptions.

Assumptions create risk.

Verification reduces dependency on trust.

Complete elimination of trust remains an open question.

---

## Foundation 6: Continuity Is Not Automatic

Understanding naturally degrades.

Intent naturally drifts.

Documentation naturally becomes outdated.

Continuity must be actively preserved.

---

## Foundation 7: Repositories Can Preserve Understanding

Repositories are more than code containers.

They can preserve:

- Intent
- Context
- Assumptions
- Decisions
- Questions
- Evidence
- History

A repository can become a continuity artifact.

---

## Foundation 8: Questions Generate Discovery

Many important investigations begin with simple questions.

The question often survives longer than the answer.

Questions should be preserved.

---

## Foundation 9: Governance Exists Wherever Mutation Exists

Where change is possible:

Governance exists.

Whether explicit or implicit.

Formal or informal.

Recognized or unrecognized.

Governance emerges wherever mutation authority exists.

---

## Foundation 10: Truth And Evidence Are Different

Truth may exist without evidence.

Evidence may exist without truth.

Recoverability depends upon preserving enough evidence to approach truth.

---

## Foundation 11: Understanding Has Lineage

Ideas emerge from prior ideas.

Questions emerge from prior questions.

Understanding has ancestry.

Lineage matters.

---

## Foundation 12: Learning Never Ends

Every investigation eventually uncovers additional questions.

Every answer creates new boundaries.

Every boundary creates new investigations.

The process continues.

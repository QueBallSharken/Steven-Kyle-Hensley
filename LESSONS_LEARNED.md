# Lessons Learned

This document records lessons that emerged through experience rather than theory.

Many were learned repeatedly before they were understood.

---

## Lesson 1

Understanding and agreement are not the same thing.

People can understand an idea and still disagree with it.

People can agree with an idea and still misunderstand it.

---

## Lesson 2

Documentation written too late becomes archaeology.

Documentation written during investigation preserves intent.

---

## Lesson 3

The repository is often more important than the conversation.

Conversations create momentum.

Repositories preserve continuity.

---

## Lesson 4

Questions are often more valuable than answers.

A good question can survive for years.

An answer may survive only until new evidence appears.

---

## Lesson 5

Complexity accumulates naturally.

Simplicity requires effort.

---

## Lesson 6

Most ideas are not ready when they first appear.

Time, criticism, experimentation, and revision are part of the process.

---

## Lesson 7

Being early often looks the same as being wrong.

Time eventually separates the two.

---

## Lesson 8

Curiosity scales better than certainty.

Certainty resists correction.

Curiosity invites learning.

---

## Lesson 9

Repositories preserve more than code.

They preserve:

- Context
- Direction
- Decisions
- Assumptions
- Failures
- Recoverability

---

## Lesson 10

Not every abandoned path was a mistake.

Some paths exist to teach what not to build.

---

## Lesson 11

Public learning is uncomfortable.

The discomfort is often evidence that learning is occurring.

---

## Lesson 12

The investigation is larger than any individual repository.

Repositories are artifacts.

The investigation is the constant.

---

## Ongoing Lesson

The more I learn, the more I discover how much remains unknown.

# Method

This document records recurring investigation patterns that emerged through experimentation, research, public discussion, implementation, failure, revision, and continuity work.

The purpose is not to prescribe a process.

The purpose is to preserve one.

---

## Beginning

Most investigations begin the same way.

A question appears.

The question refuses to leave.

The investigation begins.

---

## Step 1: Observe

Observe before concluding.

Observe before designing.

Observe before solving.

Understanding should precede intervention whenever possible.

---

## Step 2: Ask Questions

Questions often reveal more than answers.

A useful question can expose:

- Hidden assumptions
- Missing context
- Contradictions
- Unknown dependencies
- Governance failures

Questions are often the first form of evidence.

---

## Step 3: Follow The Boundary

When uncertainty exists:

Follow the boundary.

Determine:

- What changes
- What survives
- What mutates
- Who controls mutation
- Who controls interpretation

The boundary frequently reveals the system.

---

## Step 4: Preserve Understanding

Important observations should be documented.

Memory is temporary.

Repositories are durable.

If understanding matters, preserve it.

---

## Step 5: Test Assumptions

Assumptions should be challenged.

Especially assumptions that appear obvious.

Many investigations begin where obvious assumptions fail.

---

## Step 6: Search For Survivors

Pay attention to ideas that survive:

- Criticism
- Implementation
- Translation
- Stress
- Time

Survivability contains information.

---

## Step 7: Follow Contradictions

Contradictions are often valuable.

When two things appear simultaneously true and incompatible:

Investigate.

The contradiction may reveal hidden structure.

---

## Step 8: Reduce To A Question

Complex investigations often become clearer when reduced to a single question.

Examples include:

> Can we prove a decision followed policy?

and

> Can a governing invariant survive every mutation-capable boundary until the irreversible mutation authority?

Simple questions often produce deep investigations.

---

## Step 9: Preserve Continuity

An investigation should survive its investigator.

Important information includes:

- Current status
- Decisions
- Assumptions
- Open questions
- Historical context

Continuity should not depend on memory.

---

## Step 10: Continue Learning

No investigation is ever truly complete.

New evidence appears.

New understanding appears.

New questions appear.

The investigation continues.

---
